Lab 1 · Session 1 · Flaw of Averages

The 45-Minute Turnaround: Murtala Muhammed, 06:40 bank

A narrow-body turn has seven ground tasks. Planning with the averages puts departure at 42 minutes, comfortably inside the slot. Run 1,000 turns and see what really happens.

🔮 Predict first: with a 45-min slot, what % of turns leave on time?

Criticality index: how often each task drives the departure
The turn network
The Flaw of Averages. Departure is the maximum of several parallel paths. The average of a maximum is always bigger than the maximum of the averages, so a plan built from averages is biased early. It is not merely uncertain. Notice also that the task that looks critical on paper (refuelling) is often not the one that drives delay. Invest where the criticality index points, not where the Gantt chart points.
Lab 2 · Session 1 · Utilisation × Variability

Live floor
Queue length over time
The hockey stick: expected wait vs utilisation
Kingman's law: wait ≈ variability × utilisation ÷ (1 − utilisation) × service time. Going from 80% to 90% busy roughly doubles waiting time, and from 90% to 95% doubles it again. Halving variability (scheduled slots, standard work, digital pre-checks) buys as much as adding a server. Most efficiency programmes push utilisation up and then wonder why queues explode.

December Demand Shock: the bullwhip in a four-tier chain

Open-market retailers → Distributor → Regional depot → Factory. Shoppers' demand rises during the festive season. Each tier orders sensibly from its own point of view, yet the factory sees chaos.

Structure creates behaviour. Nobody here is irrational. The bullwhip comes from delays plus decision rules that forget the supply line: "I ordered last week but it hasn't arrived, so I'll order again." Long lead times amplify it. The cures are structural: count the pipeline, share POS data, shorten lead times and correct gently. Click "Disciplined planner" to see it vanish.
Lab 4 · Session 2 · Reliability Under Uncertainty

The Asset Fleet: when should you maintain?

A fleet of 24 critical assets over 3 years. Failure times follow a Weibull distribution. The shape β tells you how things fail, and that decides which maintenance strategy pays.

🔮 Predict: will preventive maintenance every 180 days save money on this asset?
Cost per asset-year vs PM interval (₦m)
Preventive at interval TRun to failureCondition-based
Fleet timeline: 24 assets × 3 years (● failure · ◆ planned intervention)
The shape decides the strategy. β < 1: infant mortality, so PM makes things worse by resetting good assets into the risky early period. β ≈ 1: random failures, where calendar PM is pure waste and only condition monitoring helps. β > 1: wear-out, where an optimal interval exists. Condition-based maintenance wins whenever sensors detect failures early enough. That's why the data layer (CMMS + sensors) must come before the AI model.

Disruptions fatten the tail, and the tail sets your buffer. Safety stock formulas built on "average lead time ± a bit" collapse when 1 shipment in 5 is 4 weeks late. The gap between the two curves is the resilience premium: inventory you carry purely because of port and FX risk. Put a naira value on it. That's the business case for dual sourcing, bonded stock or a Lagos Free Zone buffer warehouse.
